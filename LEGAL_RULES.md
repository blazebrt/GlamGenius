# Writing Rules — every user-facing string

Context: In Mondelez vs Himatsingka the Delhi High Court held that a
critic "can comment on facts but can't belittle" a product. Facts are
protected. Characterisation is not.

## The six rules

1. STATE, DO NOT CHARACTERISE
   YES: "Added sugar: 32g per 100g."
   NO:  "Shockingly high sugar."

2. CITE, DO NOT ASSERT
   YES: "Above the ICMR-NIN 2024 threshold for high sugar."
   NO:  "This is unhealthy."

3. COMPARE PRODUCTS, NEVER ADVISE THE PERSON
   YES: "Glycinate absorbs 5-6x better than oxide."
   NO:  "You should take glycinate."

4. NO MOCKERY, EVER
   No wit at a brand's expense. Not in verdicts, notifications, share
   cards or campaign copy.

5. ABSENCE OF DATA IS STATED, NEVER FILLED
   YES: "Not enough information — proceed with caution."
   NO:  Any confident guess.

6. EVERY NEGATIVE STATEMENT CARRIES ITS SOURCE, VISIBLE WITHOUT TAPPING

## Plain language
Understandable by an average Indian adult with basic English. No
technical terms on any primary screen.

Never show          Show instead
------------------  ----------------------------------
Ultra-processed     Factory-made
NOVA Group 4        (never shown)
INS 322             A chemical added to keep it mixed
Saturated fat 12g   Made with palm oil
Sodium 890mg        Very salty
Bioavailability     How much your body takes in
Sugar 24g/100g      6 spoons of sugar in one packet
NAQI 340            Air is very bad today

Convert numbers into things people already picture: spoons of sugar,
rupees, "a full day's salt in one packet".

The primary verdict sentence must be under ten words and must say what
to DO, not what the thing IS.

## Never show a reading where a decision belongs
YES: "Move tonight's exfoliant to Saturday. Air is very bad and the
      weather is dry — your skin barrier is under strain this week."
NO:  "Temp 12C · Humidity 34% · UV 6 · AQI 340"

## The test
Write every string as though it will be read aloud in the Delhi High
Court with the brand's senior counsel present.

## Never hard-code a user-facing string
Every string lives in a keyed string file from day one.
